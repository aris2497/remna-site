What is deadstock fabric?

A short guide to deadstock: what it is, why it is worth sewing with, and how to buy it.

Deadstock fabric is surplus cloth that was produced but never used: rolls ordered by fashion houses and brands that ended up unused, overruns from mills, and the last metres of a collection. It is usually first-quality fabric — silk, wool, linen, cotton, cashmere and blends — sold in limited quantities, often well below the price of newly made cloth.

Why sew with deadstock?

Using deadstock gives existing fabric a second life instead of letting it go to waste, and it lets small brands and home sewists work with materials normally reserved for large fashion houses. Because quantities are limited, many deadstock fabrics are one-offs: when a roll sells out, it is gone.

How is deadstock sold?

Shops sell deadstock by the metre, by the half metre, or as fixed cuts (coupons) of a given length. Some list the composition, width and weight (g/m²); others only give a name. Remna gathers these details in one place and converts every price to price per metre, so cuts and rolls from different shops are easy to compare.

What to check before you buy

Look at the composition (for example 100% silk or a blend with elastane), the width (wider fabric means fewer metres for the same garment), the weight (light fabrics for blouses and linings, heavier ones for trousers and coats) and how many metres are left. Order a swatch when a shop offers one, especially for colour.
